This project is part of a wider programme coordinated by Frontier Climate to study ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE).

The Cambridge team, including [John Taylor](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/jrt51/) in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics (DAMTP) and [Oscar Branson](/about) in the Department of Earth Sciences, will build a high-resolution model of the near-field environment surrounding the site of the 2025 OAE field trial conducted by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution. The team will work closely with [Dr Adam Subhas](https://subhaslab.whoi.edu/) and other colleagues to investigate potential alkalinity loss through secondary mineral precipitation. The simulations will also help assess sampling strategies.

Simulations will use [Oceananigans.jl](https://github.com/Oceananigans) to represent physical processes at the field site and [OceanBioME.jl](https://github.com/OceanBioME) to model how dissolving and sinking particles interact with seawater carbon chemistry. Experience in ocean modelling, particularly with Oceananigans, is highly desirable. Knowledge of seawater carbon chemistry and mineral formation/dissolution is desirable but not essential.

The postdoc will be based in Cambridge, UK, and will be expected to travel to the US for collaboration.
